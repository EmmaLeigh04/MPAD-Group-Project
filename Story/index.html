In early March 2005, a local teenager goes missing and is later found dead.

Being from a small urban town where everyone knew everyone, Monica Jones’ picture was in every newspaper and on every street pole.

Her death was ruled an accident. Monica, the first person in her grade to get their driver’s licence, just happened to crash into that pole.

The case was closed, and the town moved on.

Except for Summer

********* 21 Years Later *********

Monica’s parents finally decided to let go of their family home and move across the country after 21 years.

Her soul haunts the place, forever stuck at 17.

They invite Monica’s once best friend, Summer, over to go through her perfectly preserved room for any keepsakes before they let go of it all and finally move on.

*

At one point in time, wherever Monica went, Summer was one step behind her. They were inseparable.

Everyone in school knew them as a pair. Monica was the popular, outgoing girl everyone loved. And Summer, by association, was pretty cool too.

Summer never really let go of Monica’s death. She always felt there was something wrong with it all, something the cops were missing. Or deliberately overlooking.

*

Summer walks up the steps to 573 Pixie Boulevard one last time. Memories flood back with every step she takes.

She rings the doorbell, but the door swings open immediately.

“Summer! It’s so great to see you, sweetie,” Mrs. Jones says as she goes in for a big hug. Summer hugging her back reluctantly.

She ushers Summer inside, offering tea and her famous homemade oatmeal cookies, the ones Monica loved as a child.

The house smells just as it always did. A mix of nutty, browned butter from Mrs. Jones’ baking, and tobacco from Mr. Jones’ cigars. Mr. Jones was sat in his usual spot, his big green Lazboy armchair, reading some historical, non-fiction literature. A classic, he always said.

They tell Summer to take whatever she pleases and leave her to it.

*

Monica’s room is a time capsule, frozen in 2005.

Summer walks around Monica’s childhood bedroom, every nook and cranny bringing up old memories. Their childhoods, and secrets, engraved into the pink walls forever.

Blink-182 and Evanescence band posters are still pasted on every wall; her low-waisted jeans and halter tops still hang in the closet; a No Doubt CD is still loaded into her portable CD player.

Summer stops for a moment, letting the memories flood back and then, as if two decades hadn’t passed since last stepping into Monica’s room, walks over to the laundry basket, and takes that pair of jeans she always loved.

As she picks them up, she hears the crinkling of paper - a password in the pocket.. She glances back towards Monica's old desktop. That computer knew Monica's life better than anybody.

Summer knew that if there was some missing clue that could help reopen Monica's case, it would be in that computer.

With fingers crossed, praying the laptop would turn on, Summer pressed the power button.
